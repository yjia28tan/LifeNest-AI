## Why these schemes exist

Many first-time buyers can afford the monthly instalment on a modest home but cannot assemble the down payment and upfront costs. Others have irregular income that a bank finds hard to verify. Malaysian federal and state governments, together with banks and government-linked agencies, run schemes aimed at these gaps. Their names, terms and funding change over time, so this note describes the common types rather than the current terms of any particular scheme.

## Common types of assistance

**Credit guarantee schemes.** A government-backed guarantor covers part of the loan, which lets a bank lend more than it otherwise would. This can raise the margin of finance above the usual level and reduce the down payment a buyer needs. Some guarantee schemes are aimed at buyers without a fixed monthly salary, such as gig workers and small traders.

**Stamp duty exemptions.** First-time buyers may receive full or partial exemption from stamp duty on the memorandum of transfer and the loan agreement, up to a price ceiling and within a set period.

**Affordable housing programmes.** Federal and state programmes offer homes at controlled prices to eligible buyers, often with resale restrictions for a period after purchase.

**Developer and bank packages.** Some developers absorb legal fees or offer rebates, and some banks offer special first-home financing. These are commercial offers, and their true value should be compared with a standard loan.

## Typical eligibility dimensions

Most schemes test some combination of the following:

- **First-property status.** The buyer, and sometimes their spouse, must not already own residential property.
- **Household income ceiling.** Combined monthly household income must fall below a stated limit.
- **Property price ceiling.** The property must cost no more than a stated limit.
- **Citizenship and age.** Applicants are usually Malaysian citizens within an age range.
- **Property type and use.** The home must be residential and owner-occupied.

## How to use this information

A scheme can change what a buyer can afford, sometimes significantly. Because terms change and eligibility depends on details only the scheme operator can confirm, LifeNest AI does not apply any scheme automatically in its calculations. Its figures assume a standard loan with no exemptions or guarantees. A buyer who may qualify should check current terms with the relevant ministry, agency or bank, and then compare the result with LifeNest's standard figure.
